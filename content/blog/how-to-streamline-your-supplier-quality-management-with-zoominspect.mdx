## The Purpose of Conducting a Supplier Audit

An impartial and entirely autonomous evaluation of a supplier's operations and supplies concerning the specified criterion - such as company policies or global guidelines - is termed a Supplier Audit. It might be as simple as an audit of a particular production phase or perhaps as intricate as an audit of the entire organization, yet the main goal is to determine strategies to enhance a product, procedure, or structure. Although audits are perceived as being tedious and unsettling, they are generally unambiguous, productive, and essentially advantageous practices.

Audits assess every aspect of a supply chain and initiate measures to rectify the discovered shortcomings. The purpose of audits is to uncover prospective risks ahead of time and to pinpoint concerns that already exist and require rectification. Therefore, it is encouraged that audits take place at frequent intervals since they serve as an essential resource for Supplier Quality Risk Management.

## The Classification of Supplier Audits

Supplier audits fall into four main areas, each with a distinct emphasis. We will go into great detail about each of these categories in this part, outlining how they contribute to maintaining high standards of efficiency and excellence throughout the supply chain.

### 1. Audits of Management System

Instead of concentrating on particular elements or operations, a system audit assesses the efficacy and efficiency of a management system altogether. Their purpose is to analyze the supply chain's staff, procedures, locations, and the order of tasks to predetermined standards. These audits offer an in-depth overview to assist you in identifying any aspects of your supply chain structure that demand improvement.

### 2. Audits of Procedures

Process audits thoroughly examine individual processes throughout the supply chain. Productivity or outcomes regarding the preceding phase are taken into consideration while evaluating each phase of the procedure. A framework of specifications, regulations, evaluation metrics, and directions serves to analyze and compare all the necessary elements required to generate the outcome, including raw supplies, labor, and equipment.

### 3. Audits of Products

Before a produced item gets delivered to a sales location or intended consumer, it is audited to make sure it meets all requirements. Dimensional uniformity, visual impact, and operational efficiency are all featured in the parameters. A product audit may reveal that the entire quality control system demands modification, or it may highlight a single non-compliant procedure phase.

### 4. Audits for Quality Compliance

The purpose of compliance audits varies slightly from the other three categories of audits. The system, procedure, and product audits are grounded in internally established specifications and serve as a means of mutual feedback. Compliance audits, on the other hand, validate or reject to align product and procedure specifications with documented evidence. It offers an unambiguous method of determining whether or not requirements are fulfilled. An external specification such as national policies might also be mandated, in which case the supply chain may require more extensive modifications. Audits of the food supply, ecological sustainability, and code of conduct are examples of popular compliance audits.

## Phases of an Effective Supplier Audit

- Establish Scope and Objectives: Determine the scope of the audit, its objectives, and the guidelines that will be used as the benchmark during the audit process.
- Create an Audit Plan: This entails creating a thorough audit plan and a checklist to guide the auditing procedure. The audit plan ought to encompass the duration of the audit, the composition of the audit team along with the roles and responsibilities of each team member.
- Conduct an Initial Evaluation: This involves a preliminary evaluation of the supplier to determine potential risks, select critical suppliers for audit, and gather relevant documentation.
- Schedule and Conduct the Audit: Arrange interviews with the suppliers along with onsite inspections are mandatory to make sure the supplier adheres to all applicable laws and regulations.
- Confirm, Report, and Follow-Up: Following the audit, the supplier must submit all necessary documentation. Non-compliance, evaluations, and recommendations for enhancing quality control must all be included in the final report. Subsequent audits must include an evaluation of the supplier's remediation strategy.
- Evaluate and Manage Suppliers: The outcomes of the supplier audit must be taken into account when assessing the reliability and performance of the supplier. It is essential to create mitigation plans for non-compliance issues and to conduct periodic evaluations to monitor supplier performance.
